# FOSCOD Landing-Page Research and Content Strategy

**Research date:** 6 August 2026  
**Purpose:** Give FOSCOD's priority audiences enough context, trust, and practical detail to choose an appropriate next step.  
**Internal evidence base:** FOSCOD 2026 Online Audit Report; FOSCOD Strategic Plan 2026–2030 and annexes; approved website rebuild brief.  

## Executive summary

FOSCOD's strongest public position is not simply that it delivers projects or hosts visitors. It is that FOSCOD connects locally defined priorities in Uganda with responsible learning, technical expertise, funding, and institutional collaboration.

The website should answer four questions early on every important landing page:

1. Is this for me?
2. Why does this work matter in this place?
3. How is the work kept locally led, ethical, and accountable?
4. What happens after I respond?

The research also shows that trust is audience-specific. A student needs role scope, supervision, cost, preparation, and safety information. A university needs learning outcomes, duty of care, research ethics, and an MOU pathway. A funder needs governance, delivery capacity, budget logic, evidence, and safeguards. A community stakeholder needs plain language about who decides, what participation does and does not guarantee, consent, feedback, and whom to contact.

## Recommended positioning

**Organization-level promise**

> FOSCOD connects community priorities in Uganda with people and institutions ready to learn, fund, research, or deliver responsibly.

**Global Learning & Exchange promise**

> Learn in Uganda while contributing your skills to priorities communities and local partners already lead.

**CEDP promise**

> Connect national climate and development priorities with practical, locally owned action in Buikwe District.

**Partner promise**

> Co-design locally led development with clear roles, realistic costs, appropriate safeguards, and evidence agreed from the start.

**Support promise**

> Begin with a named project or priority, then confirm its current need, use of funds, transfer route, and reporting arrangement before contributing.

## Audience decision matrix

| Audience | Their first questions | Proof and detail required | Best first action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Student or early-career applicant | Is there a real role? Am I qualified? What will I learn and do? | Role purpose, tasks and limits, deliverables, supervisor, dates, fees, accommodation, support and eligibility | Check your fit |
| Volunteer | Is the work genuinely needed? Will I be useful and safe? | Community origin of role, skill match, conduct and safeguarding, handover, continuity and transparent fee brief | See verified open roles / register interest |
| Faculty or education-abroad team | Does this meet academic, risk and accessibility requirements? | Learning outcomes, assessment, itinerary, cohort parameters, duty of care, emergency roles, budget and review | Request a co-design conversation |
| Researcher | Is the question locally relevant and the partnership equitable? | Ethics review, local governance, data ownership, authorship, compensation, dissemination and return of findings | Propose community-aligned research |
| Funder or grantmaker | Is FOSCOD legitimate, capable and accountable? | Registration, governance, budget, financial controls, safeguarding, MEL, evidence, sustainability and references | Request a tailored concept or due-diligence conversation |
| NGO, CBO or technical partner | Will ownership, credit, overhead and risk be shared fairly? | Joint agenda, roles, costs, attribution, risk, reporting, learning and feedback | Explore a consortium or technical partnership |
| Local government | Does this complement public priorities and avoid duplication? | Legal identity, geography, coordination route, existing MoU, roles, standards, referrals and reporting | Schedule a coordination conversation |
| Community stakeholder | Who decides, who benefits, and how can I raise a concern? | Current geography, participation expectations, consent, privacy, feedback and contact route | Request a community conversation |
| Individual supporter | Where will support go and how will I know? | Named project, current status, approved payment route, use of funds, reporting, identity and privacy | Start a giving inquiry |

## Content architecture applied to the rebuild

### Home

- Audience pathway selector before asking visitors to decode program names.
- Two program routes retained: GLE and CEDP.
- Four-step responsible-engagement explanation.
- Verified impact and named project content remain distinct from wider context.
- Final action offers programs, partnership, and application routes.

### Programs

- Program choice by purpose and by audience.
- Theory of change remains the shared program logic.
- Institutional and funder route no longer hides behind an individual program finder.

### Global Learning & Exchange

- Four pathways: internships, responsible volunteering, faculty-led programs, and community-based research.
- Ethical workflow: listen and define; match and prepare; work with guidance; hand over; review together.
- Practical FAQ covers role origin, experience, support, academic credit, safeguarding, and community benefit.
- “Nationwide reach” removed because a program overview should not imply a current supervised placement everywhere.
- Institutional research language now requires advance agreement on ethics, data, authorship, dissemination, and local feedback.

### Community Empowerment & Development

- Six themes explained as an integrated system, not a list of activities.
- National sources appear as wider context only.
- Five-stage community-led project process is explicit.
- Each sub-program now includes: why the issue matters, progress markers, partner fit, responsible evidence limits, external source, and 2030 targets.

### Partnerships

- Six audience-specific routes replace broad partnership labels.
- Registration, address, MoU and verified organization-wide results form the first trust layer.
- Five-stage process moves from fit to joint review.
- FAQ introduces governance, due diligence, claims, data, budget, risk, and responsibilities before a concept is promised.

### Donations and support

- Named projects replace vague theme-only giving cards.
- Giving remains an inquiry until FOSCOD confirms a current need and approved transfer method.
- Donor stewardship process is visible.
- Carbon support is explicitly separated from an offset claim.

### Internships and volunteering

- “Anyone can help” language is replaced by skill and role matching.
- Preparation and handover are part of the participant journey.
- Fees and support are confirmed through a current, version-dated brief rather than broad permanent promises.
- FAQs address competence, academic credit or employment, safeguarding, accessibility, costs, privacy, supervision, and continuity.

## Proof architecture

Every important claim should live in one of five visible layers:

1. **Wider challenge:** national or international evidence, with source and date.
2. **What FOSCOD does:** verified program method and approved geography.
3. **FOSCOD-wide track record:** approved 2019–2024 figures, clearly labelled organization-wide.
4. **Project-specific results:** linked to the relevant project record, period, definition, evidence, and limitations.
5. **Future targets:** labelled “2030 target” or “planned,” never presented as achieved.

Required CMS fields for a result should include metric definition, value, unit, geography, period, as-of date, evidence source, verification status, linked project, and limitations. Personal stories require recorded consent. External context must never populate FOSCOD impact counters.

## Responsible-claims guide

### Global learning

- Say “intended learning outcomes,” not guaranteed career outcomes.
- State that the home institution decides academic credit.
- Publish a role only when purpose, tasks, prohibited activities, outputs, supervisor, timing, requirements, and status are verified.
- Avoid unsupervised child-facing roles and work beyond a participant's competence.
- Treat participant satisfaction and host-community benefit as different measures.

### Green skills and livelihoods

- Say “build skills and pathways toward work or enterprise,” not guarantee jobs.
- Separate people trained, enterprises started, enterprise survival, income, and environmental outcomes.
- Distinguish community awareness or introductory training from accredited technical certification.

### Clean cooking and health

- Use “clean for health” only for fuel-and-technology combinations that meet WHO emissions targets.
- Use “improved” or “lower-emission” where performance does not meet that threshold.
- Do not claim disease prevention without project-level health evidence.
- Monitor continued use and stove stacking; distribution is not adoption.
- Keep household health evidence separate from carbon estimates.

### WASH

- Do not call water safe without current quality testing.
- Separate infrastructure completion from functionality, safe service, use, maintenance, time savings, and health outcomes.
- Include governance, accessibility, hygiene, sanitation, repair, and water-quality plans.

### Inclusion and leadership

- Attendance is not empowerment.
- Measure speaking roles, decision-making, resources controlled, initiatives completed, and changes influenced.
- Consider language, transport, disability access, care responsibilities, safety, and feedback routes.

### Restoration and carbon finance

- Tree planting is not the same as restored forest; monitor survival and ecosystem function.
- The Carbon Credit Project remains planned until its public status changes.
- Estimated reductions are not credits. Credits require the applicable approval, validation, monitoring, independent verification, issuance, registry traceability, transfer, and retirement.
- Do not use Gold Standard or Verra certification language or logos before the registry confirms that exact status.
- State land, tree and carbon rights, informed participation, grievance routes, permanence risk, and benefit-sharing arrangements when verified.

## Due-diligence content backlog

Before making related public claims, FOSCOD should verify and approve current versions of:

- NGO permit and latest annual-return evidence
- latest annual report and audited accounts
- board-approved budget or public financial summary
- safeguarding/PSEA and child-safeguarding policies
- code of conduct and complaints/feedback procedure
- privacy notice, data-protection contact and applicable PDPO evidence
- anti-fraud, conflict-of-interest and procurement controls
- partnership principles and standard MOU/work-order template
- current GLE health, safety, emergency, insurance, accommodation and accessibility brief
- current program fees, inclusions, exclusions and refund terms
- research ethics, data, authorship and return-of-findings template

If a current approved document is unavailable, omit the compliance claim or state that the item is being verified. Do not create a badge or implied certification.

## Limitations

- National and international sources describe the wider problem and good practice; they do not establish a Buikwe baseline or prove FOSCOD impact.
- Some research evidence on global internships is self-reported and affected by participant self-selection. The website therefore describes learning intentions without promising academic, career, or employment outcomes.
- Website research could not verify every current FOSCOD policy, price, intake, support service, partner document, or financial statement. The copy directs users to obtain a current verified brief.
- Carbon-market standards and Ugandan rules can change. Project-status and buyer-claim language requires review at the time of publication or transaction.
- External links should be reviewed periodically for currency and accessibility.
