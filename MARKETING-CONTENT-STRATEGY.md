# FOSCOD Website Marketing and Content Strategy

**Version:** 6 August 2026  
**Primary conversion goal:** Generate qualified, values-aligned partnership, program, and giving inquiries.  
**Guardrail:** Meaningful conversion matters more than inquiry volume. The website should help unsuitable applicants self-select out and help serious partners prepare a better first message.

## Offer and audience

FOSCOD has two connected offers:

- **Global Learning & Exchange (GLE):** supervised, community-connected learning for interns, volunteers, faculty groups, institutions, and researchers.
- **Community Empowerment & Development Program (CEDP):** locally led work across green skills and renewable energy, clean cooking and health, WASH, green livelihoods, inclusive leadership, and ecosystem restoration.

Priority audiences:

1. Ugandan communities, CBOs, and public institutions
2. Universities, faculty teams, researchers, and education-abroad offices
3. Students, graduates, early-career professionals, and responsible volunteers
4. Grantmakers, individual supporters, INGOs, and technical partners
5. Responsible businesses and future climate-finance partners

## Positioning

**Core message:** Community priorities lead. FOSCOD then connects the appropriate learning, skills, funding, technology, and partnerships to the work.

**What makes FOSCOD distinct:**

- Ugandan identity, leadership, registration, and physical presence
- a place-based CEDP model in five named Buikwe communities
- a Global Learning program designed to connect participant learning with community value
- verified 2019–2024 organizational evidence separated from 2030 targets
- an explicit commitment to consent, role boundaries, locally guided delivery, and responsible claims

Avoid positioning based on “saving,” “giving back,” poverty imagery, guaranteed transformation, instant impact, or invented urgency.

## Brand voice

Use a voice that is:

- **Locally grounded:** name the place, community role, and practical reality.
- **Specific:** explain what happens, who decides, and what evidence means.
- **Warm but not sentimental:** welcome contribution without centering the outsider.
- **Confident but careful:** publish verified facts and label plans, targets, and limitations.
- **Plain-spoken:** translate development, research, WASH, carbon, and safeguarding terms.
- **Action-oriented:** every page ends with a relevant next step.

Preferred language:

- “work with communities”
- “community-defined priority”
- “locally guided”
- “verified 2019–2024 result”
- “2030 target”
- “planned project”
- “check your fit”
- “start a conversation”
- “confirm the current brief”

Language to avoid unless exact evidence supports it:

- “life-changing”
- “nationwide reach”
- “anyone can volunteer”
- “guaranteed employment”
- “safe water” without current testing
- “clean for health” for unqualified stove technology
- “carbon credits” before issuance
- “certified” or “compliant” without current proof

## Conversion journeys

### Individual applicant

Home audience path → internship or volunteer overview → ethical process and FAQ → verified open role or interest route → fit/application form → human review.

Primary CTA: **Check your fit**  
Secondary CTA: **Review the current program brief**

### University or researcher

Home/Programs → GLE pathway selector → ethical and CBPR principles → partnership page → co-design inquiry → due-diligence and program brief exchange.

Primary CTA: **Request a co-design conversation**  
Secondary CTA: **Discuss community-aligned research**

### Funder or institutional partner

Home/Programs → CEDP overview or theme page → wider context + verified FOSCOD evidence → partner page → inquiry → concept and due diligence.

Primary CTA: **Start a partnership inquiry**  
Secondary CTA: **Review verified impact**

### Individual supporter

Home/Impact/project page → support page → named project options + stewardship → giving inquiry → approved transfer route and reporting agreement.

Primary CTA: **Start a giving inquiry**  
Secondary CTA: **Check our identity**

### Community or public-sector stakeholder

Home/Programs → CEDP/geography → contact or partner route → community/coordination message → local follow-up.

Primary CTA: **Request a community conversation**  
Secondary CTA: **Schedule technical coordination**

## Content operations

### Publish only when ready

- Open opportunity: verified role, status, supervisor, eligibility, dates, outputs, costs and review owner.
- Result: value, definition, period, source, status and limitation.
- Story: consent, attribution preference, evidence, dignity review and image rights.
- Partner: approved name, relationship description, logo permission and current status.
- Program fee: version date, currency, inclusions, exclusions, refund/cancellation reference and owner.
- Policy or due-diligence file: approval date, version, owner and review date.

### Useful editorial cadence

- Monthly: verify active roles, dates, fees, forms, contact details and broken links.
- Quarterly: review landing-page questions, inquiry quality, search terms, source currency and project status.
- Annually: update verified metrics, annual documents, governance, partner records, policies and strategic-target progress.
- Immediately: update safety, safeguarding, legal, payment, privacy, outbreak, travel, or carbon-status information when authoritative guidance changes.

## Measurement

Measure conversion quality, not only form volume:

- completed fit applications / started applications
- applications meeting minimum published requirements
- partnership inquiries containing organization, goal, timing, contribution, and evidence needs
- inquiry-to-discovery-call rate by audience
- discovery-to-concept and concept-to-agreement rates
- project-specific giving inquiries and confirmed contributions
- FAQ and program-brief engagement before form submission
- form response time and follow-up completion
- reasons unsuitable applicants or partners self-select out
- community/partner feedback on clarity, burden, and fairness

Do not interpret website conversion as community impact. Marketing, delivery, learning, and community outcomes require separate indicators.
